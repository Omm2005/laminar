Method

Four moves. One of them is hard.

Lifting and carrying a vial is solved. Knowing when to stop pouring is not. So everything up to the pour runs on fixed, measured poses, and the camera only has to do the one job that needs it.

01 / ACQUIRE

The finger bends, not the tube

Fin-ray fingers close to an 11 mm gap. Press a tube into the flat face and the whole finger curls around it, so a stalled motor flexes plastic instead of crushing a 0.9 mm wall.

02 / TRANSPORT

The wrist never moves

Six joints carry the vial across the bench with the wrist roll locked at zero. The meniscus never breaks, so the volume measured at the rack is the volume that arrives.

03 / ALIGN

Look first, then commit

A camera on the gripper, pitched 38° out over the fingertips, picks up the marker on the station and corrects the approach before the arm descends. Move the station mid-demo; it still finds it.

04 / POUR

One joint, closed loop

Only the wrist rolls. A second camera reads the rising column in the channel and feeds volume back five times a second, so the tilt stops on a measured volume, not a stopwatch.

The adaptive pour

A tilt angle gets you a pour. It doesn't get you a volume.

Tip the same vial twice, to the same angle, for the same two seconds, and you get two different volumes. Surface tension, how full the tube started, how cleanly the lip broke: all of it moves the number.

So the angle is not the setpoint. The volume is. The wrist rolls in, the station camera measures the column climbing in the channel, and the controller trims the tilt until the delivered volume reaches target, then rolls back out and holds.

Hardware

Stock arm. Everything that touches glass, we printed.

ARM

reBot B601-RS

Seven RobStride joints on a 1 Mbps CAN bus, driven straight from a laptop through a USB-CAN adapter. No vendor stack anywhere in the loop.

GRIP

Fin-ray fingers

Hollow triangles with five fanned ribs. The contact wall bows inward under load and the tip swings in with it, so the finger closes around the tube rather than onto it.

EYES

38° gripper mount

One printed piece that slides onto the gripper block and stops against the frame. Holds the camera out over the fingertips, where the vial lip sits 5° above frame centre.

BENCH

One-piece station

Reservoir and measuring camera share a single tray, so moving the station never changes the calibration. Hand it to a judge and let them put it anywhere.
